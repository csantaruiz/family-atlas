# Family Atlas — Vision

## What It Is

Family Atlas is an interactive, museum-like experience for exploring family history through time. It is not a genealogy database with a chart bolted on. It is a curated spatial narrative: a place to wander, discover, and feel the weight and texture of a family's story as it unfolds against the backdrop of world history.

The experience should feel like walking through a quiet gallery — deliberate pacing, generous space, and moments of revelation rather than information overload.

## Core Experience

A visitor enters a dark, textured environment and encounters a horizontal timeline that spans generations. Above the axis live **family events** — births, deaths, migrations, military service, occupations, and family stories. Below the axis live **world-history events** that provide cultural and historical context for the family's journey.

Interaction is exploratory and progressive:

- **Semantic zoom** reveals more detail as the visitor moves closer in time.
- **Clusters** resolve crowded periods into navigable groups; clicking a cluster always zooms inward.
- A **highlight carousel** surfaces curated family stories with warm, tinted cards and subtle transitions.
- A **detail panel** opens on demand without displacing the visitor from the timeline.

## Data Philosophy

The uploaded **GEDCOM file** will eventually be the canonical source of family data. All family events, relationships, and biographical facts derive from GEDCOM (with editorial layers for stories and curation). World-history context is a separate, curated layer that enriches but does not override family truth.

## Audience

Family Atlas is built for families who want to *experience* their history — not just query it. The primary audience includes:

- Family historians who have already gathered GEDCOM data and want a beautiful way to share it.
- Extended family members who would never open a genealogy app but would explore a museum-like interface.
- Future generations who inherit the atlas as a living artifact.

## Success Criteria

- A first-time visitor understands the timeline within seconds, without instruction.
- Family and world-history layers are visually distinct yet harmoniously composed.
- No event overlaps at any zoom level; clustering handles density gracefully.
- Detail never requires leaving the timeline context.
- The experience feels calm, warm, and dignified — never cluttered or gamified.

## Non-Goals (for now)

- Real-time collaborative editing.
- Social sharing or public profiles.
- Replacing professional genealogy software for research workflows.
- Animating every element; motion is restrained and purposeful.
